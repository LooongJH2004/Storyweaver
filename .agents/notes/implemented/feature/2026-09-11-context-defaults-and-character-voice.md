# Agent Note: Initial context defaults and character expression

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-09-11-context-defaults-and-character-voice.zh.md)

## Problem

Common performance guidance can turn distinct characters into speakers who explain evidence, negotiate permission and repeat proposals. Changing the system's initial context must also preserve configurations already owned by books and instances.

## Decision

`StorybookLibrary.contextDefaults` and `saveContextDefaults` own a revision-checked recipe in the library defaults namespace. New schema-v6 drafts without an explicit recipe receive a copy; explicit imported recipes remain intact. Published versions and instances never follow this setting. Existing drafts that omit a recipe retain their historical implicit values when saved, and omitted recipes in old versions use a frozen historical recipe. Missing performance modules likewise retain their historical interpretation. Reads do not write or upgrade configuration.

The sidebar opens “Context defaults and book sharing”. “System initial context” edits the full recipe, with separate save and discard/reload actions; unsaved edits survive navigation. Book-shared modules retain their own BookId scope and opt-in instance following. Changing system defaults neither publishes shared modules nor changes their subscribers. [Book sharing](2026-09-10-shared-creative-settings.md) owns that mechanism.

Actor guidance preserves the selected length preference while replacing the mandatory response/reason/proposal sequence with character-dependent contributions. Vocabulary, rhetorical habits and priorities come from the role and relationship. Runtime instructions separate private execution rules, personal records, scene directions and fictional communication. Knowledge attribution still limits submitted records, and ownership and settlement remain enforced by their existing applications. Characters may express in-world concerns about danger, property or consent when motivated by the scene.

The shared actor submission schema describes memories as personally significant experiences whose meaning persists beyond the current scene. Newness alone does not justify copying routine events into private memory. Actor execution guidance preserves the source and conditional status of another person's words when retelling them; a conditional repair proposal is not a settled verdict. Entries in one submitted turn cannot include another person's reply between them, so asking someone to cooperate does not authorize a dependent action in that same turn. Creative guidance favors a character's next distinct choice over repeating another speaker's warning or announcing every received fact. These are model instructions, while accepted scene history and world settlement remain authoritative.

The default treats an ordinary exchange as one conversational move, usually one to three spoken sentences, rather than a 3–10 sentence target. A character may answer, avoid part of a question, act or remain quiet according to their own stakes and relationships; no common two-step speech structure is prescribed. Longer contributions remain available for conflict, emotion, important decisions and several new facts. Authored length preferences and saved performance modules keep priority. This is a default copied into new recipes or explicitly restored, not a migration of existing books or instances.

Discussion preparation requests a private, revisable intention rather than a speech outline. The actor sees only its own stance and eagerness, without preparation timestamps or round counters. Public-floor instructions allow passing and ending an exchange that needs an external result. Director instructions request differentiated cues and settlement of outstanding attempts instead of repeated speculation. These instructions do not automatically close discussions or manufacture agreement.

Director creative guidance treats authored beats as candidate pressures and opportunities. It carries consequences of actual choices into the next situation and frames new problems through a person with a concrete need and a cost that can change. Scheduling considers each candidate's stakes and available addressee rather than assigning a shared solution; it does not require every present actor to speak. A character can remain silent when another person has already made the relevant point; the model can still fail to use these options, so recorded dialogue remains the quality check.

Discussion invitations name one issue that needs reciprocal character responses. Director execution guidance defers an invitation while pending world results or an outside person's answer determines the decision, and declines bundled status agendas that direct speech can handle. Scene cues identify an immediate aim rather than an ordered report. When settling an attempt, the Director keeps its recorded actor as the performer instead of assigning the action to another character in narration.

## Baseline evidence

The local Moonshadow Ledger book is `f51c0256-c263-483f-99ef-e892dc7ea572`, with published version `a21a44e6-68a7-4ea5-b8cf-a8423c949f46` (version 1) and draft revision 3. Neither document contains a saved context recipe. Of its two surviving instances, `story-54a615ff-1ba6-4b5b-8d1d-8be0bf7e1cb0` has recipe revision 0 and last ran on September 9. `story-5a310eb6-ecaf-482c-8b44-e24e3f56b80f` has recipe revision 4, last saved at `2026-09-10T15:53:48.785Z`, and continued generating afterward. The latter is the initialization baseline because its accepted evidence contains the reported dialogue and its requests include the saved edits.

The baseline preserves source order, enabled flags, message roles, final user-role reasoning sections, director length of 500–900 Chinese characters, and actor length of 300–600. It preserves the intent of the added actor reasoning instruction to think in character without routine tool or word-count planning. No numeric narration-length setting was saved, so the default does not enable one. Creative wording is revised for individual expression; character knowledge, the world, scene cues and runtime history are not copied into system defaults.

Recorded requests identify several reinforcing sources: the common requirement to explain reasons before proposing something; Gale's authored scholarly distinctions; scene cues requiring Gale to propose an interruptible test and Shadowheart to verify before judging and justify a choice; private intentions and accepted speech that carry those patterns into subsequent contexts. Execution-only round terminology also appears in accepted speech. The evidence supports multiple reinforcing causes, not a single provider setting or a need to make every character terse.

The task's explicit local repair applies revised creative text and four scene cues only to the identified instance, moving its recipe to revision 5 and narrative state from revision 120 to 125. Its saved actor reasoning text and historical revision-120 replay remain identical. A local before-image preserves the original configuration. The system recipe is saved separately at defaults revision 1; books, published versions, other instances and sharing subscriptions are not migrated. This repair is a recorded author operation, not startup behavior.

## Alternatives considered

- Shortening every response conceals repetition and overrides saved length and character preferences.
- Replacing every character's voice with a common natural-dialogue template removes meaningful differences.
- Applying current defaults during reads or instance creation from old versions silently changes existing books. Initialization copies and frozen omitted-value interpretation keep those owners independent.
- Sharing the whole system recipe dynamically across books couples unrelated stories. Book-scoped sharing remains an explicit separate choice.

## Consequences

The [domain tests](../../../../packages/story/roleplay-core/tests/world.spec.ts) cover revision conflicts, persistence, copied and explicit values, legacy omitted recipes, unchanged versions and instances, book-sharing isolation, and private preparation projection. The [execution test](../../../../packages/experimental/actor/tests/narrative-executor.real.spec.ts) records the model-visible instructions through the real Loader and agent loop. The [browser test](../../../../apps/web/tests/player-performance.e2e.ts) saves and reloads the system recipe while checking that existing book, story and shared values remain unchanged.

An isolated diagnostic reuses recorded Moonshadow actor inputs with the configured DeepSeek model; its outputs never enter the live story. The initial sample still repeats some established caution, and one response has malformed tool JSON. Instruction snapshots establish the delivered guidance and host behavior, not stable literary quality. Accumulated character history can preserve old habits, so continued story play remains necessary to assess variation and meaningful progress.

A later isolated three-character probe used separate instances and the same observation for each before and after the default edit. Before editing, the child made four separate spoken entries after an exploratory action; the ferryman and innkeeper were already distinct and direct. The first edited run reduced the child's speech to one entry but still produced awkward local wording in the adults. A second edited run yielded short, distinct replies and retained practical actions. Native output tokens across the three calls were 3,416 before, 2,912 in the first edited run and 2,945 in the repeat; prompt-cache reads varied by call, so these samples do not establish a cost or quality rate. The probe preserved actual execution requests, observer play and native usage under `.artifacts/voice-performance/`. It did not exercise multi-turn repetition or discussion-floor quality; the shorter default cannot guarantee natural wording in stochastic generation.

A paired rerun used one built version, full scene participants and an explicit copy of the previous default for its before instances. All six requests contained the same twelve context section IDs; the actor performance text was 906 characters before and 1,037 after. The child kept two spoken entries while becoming more focused; the ferryman fell from three to two without losing the boarding plan, and the innkeeper remained brief. The revised innkeeper call needed two model steps and the revised ferryman produced more output tokens despite shorter visible dialogue. A further two-turn ferryman pair explicitly settled the rope attempt before a passenger asked to pass over a bag. Both variants answered and repeated a safety caution; the revised answer was shorter and attempted to receive the bag. These samples confirm delivery and some visible voice changes, but rule out a blanket token-saving claim and do not measure long-running repetition.

An isolated five-character probe uses the original 40-chapter *Embers and Morningstar* plan and its opening scene. In an initial two-turn sample, characters turned tentative repair claims into attributed certainties and stored some of them as memories. The revised schema and retelling guidance were exercised through the real Loader and model, with explicit world feedback for each pending inspection. All ten actor turns committed in the corrected two-phase sample, and the visible replies did not assert unverified shop inventory. Several scene events still became private memories, and one healer action anticipated the child's compliance before her reply. The sample covers only one morning; it does not establish long-term character growth or error-free factual continuity. The probe records requests, tool calls, observer play and native usage under `.artifacts/longform-performance/`.

A later pressure scene added a sick traveler, a cart driver who owned a potentially useful axle, and urgent news from Grey Gate. A matched actor-guidance control was at least as natural as additional voice directives, so those extra directives were removed. Director probes exposed a different cause of procedural dialogue: an invitation bundled several updates into a group meeting, and later scene cues enumerated paperwork, inspection, route and messages for the protagonist to assign. The Director now defers such invitations, keeps a cue to one immediate choice, and schedules an outside addressee after an offer. A saved-boundary replay opened no group discussion; a two-continuation run led the cart driver to accept and sign the exchange, the mechanic to install the axle and the party to choose the slower road. A further replay with the focused-cue wording also completed without a group discussion, but the protagonist still addressed several people in one turn and the healer spoke too certainly about the patient's outlook. These are stochastic scenes, not proof that dialogue has lost its scripted quality. The replays still contain orderly task lists and some invented exact travel details, so voice and factual grounding remain active quality risks.

Fresh-instance voice probes compared the same five-character opening, feedback and pressure scenes. The current guidance produced 23 speech entries; an added addressee rule produced 24, a much shorter guidance 27, and character-card dialogue examples 25. Counts are descriptive, not a quality score. All variants retained some repeated safety and logistics talk, so none was promoted to the default. The dialogue-example trial was motivated by [SillyTavern's character-design documentation](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern-Docs/blob/main/Usage/Characters/characterdesign.md#examples-of-dialogue); this sample did not show a clear benefit in this application.

A separate kitchen scene exposed the stronger source of meeting-like dialogue. Forced turns for all five characters turned a soup-stained letter into route and equipment checks. A cue focused on the child's letter changed his immediate response, but the remaining forced turns still became preparations. When the Director controlled scheduling with the original book, it introduced the black-box contract and a clerk at once; adding a quiet candidate beat or a generic interpersonal default did not reliably prevent that. Adding an explicit author opening direction alongside the candidate beat kept two independent Director runs in the kitchen without a new contract or clerk. They produced 14 public rows each; the second included small interpersonal gestures rather than a task meeting. The result supports authoring the active beat and its handoff condition for a quiet opening. It does not show that every such scene will avoid procedural speech: both runs still mentioned the road or boots, and the model sometimes invented background details. Saved reports and importable story archives are under `.artifacts/longform-performance/voice-new-instance-*`.

The Director finish tool description asks for one actor when the next speaker depends on that actor's actual response, and permits batching when several actors have independent reasons to act. The style tool example no longer asks a character to explain a plan in developed paragraphs. These are model instructions without a fixed cast limit. The [reactive Director choice](2026-09-29-reactive-director-speaker-choice.md) can inspect a single accepted response in the same advance when enabled. Two earlier built-runtime kitchen replays scheduled one actor at a time and two actors together, instead of summoning all five at once. One still moved to rain and boots; the other stayed closer to the wet letter. This does not establish that semantic convergence is solved. A trial that capped finish at one actor ended the run after its first actor and would require another player advance, so that cap was removed. The pressure-scene replay ended with a provider insufficient-balance error before producing a comparable result.
